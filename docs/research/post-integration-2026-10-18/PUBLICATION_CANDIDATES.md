# Publication value and prior-art boundary

The strongest current candidate is an artifact-oriented case study: a canonical
data-only scientific receiver whose universal receiving theorem discharges the
expression/state link, accompanied by an independent exact-rational reference,
negative controls and replayable scoped scientific reports. The A→B mathematics
is deliberately elementary. Neither theorem volume nor an accepted synthetic
case establishes foundational mathematical novelty or practical scientific
adoption. A broader independently reproduced case and a careful literature
comparison are required before a novelty claim.

Relevant prior art includes:

- George C. Necula, *Proof-carrying code* (1997),
  https://doi.org/10.1145/263699.263712.
- Kumar, Myreen, Norrish and Owens, *CakeML* (2014),
  https://doi.org/10.1145/2535838.2535841.
- Becker, Zyuzin, Monat, Darulova, Myreen and Fox,
  *A Verified Certificate Checker for Finite-Precision Error Bounds in Coq and
  HOL4* (2018), https://doi.org/10.23919/FMCAD.2018.8603019.

Titles/authors/DOI metadata were retrieved from Crossref during this session.
This limited metadata lookup is not a completed systematic review or full-paper
assessment. The sources establish that proof-carrying checking, verified
implementation and numerical certificate checking are existing research areas.
PCS has not established their general guarantees anew. In particular, this
Lean --run implementation does not inherit CakeML compiler verification, and
exact rational enclosure does not establish floating-point roundoff bounds.

The independently found negative-receipt defect and intermediate gate/manifest
substitution are useful engineering evidence: a green test baseline did not
establish the receiving trust boundary. Their bounded repairs support a candid
reproducibility case study, not a universal security theorem. Existing negative
neural-update results remain historical results; this phase does not claim
accelerating recursive self-improvement or new neural experiments.
