# Mission and Governance Principles

## Mission

**Make critical computation worthy of trust, while making the methods of trust broadly accessible.**

## Institutional design principles

1. **Truth before marketing.** Claims are scoped to what evidence establishes; failed validation is published internally and, when appropriate, publicly rather than buried.
2. **Verification is not validation.** Mathematical correctness under assumptions is never presented as proof that a biological, chemical, or clinical model matches reality.
3. **Open trust root.** The certificate specification, core checker, and foundational semantics should be publicly inspectable wherever safety/legal constraints permit.
4. **Research access.** Academic/nonprofit research should have broad access to core infrastructure; enterprise revenue should subsidize this access.
5. **Publish foundational research.** The company should preserve the founder's intent to publish substantive mathematical/scientific discoveries rather than enclosing knowledge merely to create scarcity.
6. **Explicit assumptions.** Every high-consequence certificate should surface unresolved assumptions and unverified dependencies.
7. **Adversarial culture.** Teams are rewarded for finding counterexamples and invalidating overclaims.
8. **Negative results matter.** Scientifically meaningful failures should feed the research record.
9. **Commercial revenue is fuel.** Revenue exists to sustain the institution, pay people well, build infrastructure, and finance ambitious research—not as the institution's only measure of success.
10. **Long-horizon research allocation.** As revenue permits, reserve a defined research budget for work with no immediate product requirement.

## Structural options to evaluate with counsel later

- Delaware Public Benefit Corporation for the commercial entity.
- Long-term nonprofit research institute/foundation as a separate entity once scale justifies it.
- Mission-locking charter language, independent scientific advisory governance, or other mechanisms that survive founder control.

No governance structure should be finalized before qualified legal/tax review.
