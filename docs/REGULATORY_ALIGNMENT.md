# Regulatory / Standards Alignment Notes (September 2026)

This product should not market itself as automatically conferring regulatory compliance. It should instead map its evidence objects to existing verification/validation expectations.

## FDA ICH M15 — Model-Informed Drug Development

FDA issued the final **M15 General Principles for Model-Informed Drug Development** guidance in June 2026. The guidance distinguishes verification from validation/applicability and states that verification of key user-generated code, equations, and calculations should be documented and available for regulatory review. It also emphasizes reliable, reproducible, traceable computerized systems and appropriate software testing.

This is a strong fit for the initial product wedge: machine-readable evidence packages that bind code/equations/calculations, software testing, provenance, and unresolved assumptions.

Official reference: FDA, “M15 General Principles for Model-Informed Drug Development,” June 2026.

## FDA modeling and simulation

FDA describes modeling and simulation as routinely used in scientific research and regulatory decision-making and reviews M&S results submitted by industry. That supports focusing on computational evidence as an important part of regulated scientific work while remaining clear that in-silico evidence complements rather than replaces empirical evidence.

## ASME VVUQ

ASME’s VVUQ portfolio provides cross-disciplinary terminology and frameworks for verification, validation, uncertainty quantification, model credibility, and lifecycle thinking across high-consequence modeling domains.

Product implication: represent verification, validation, and uncertainty as distinct evidence types rather than a single “verified” badge.
