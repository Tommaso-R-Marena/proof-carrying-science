# Writ compositional authority — independent PCS research core

This directory contains a **kernel-checked, isolated, executable authority calculus**, not a replacement for PCS's authoritative scientific checker or the full Writ Python gateway.

## Verified properties

The [exact-source Lean 4.28 verification workflow](https://github.com/Tommaso-R-Marena/proof-carrying-science/actions/runs/42577858841) compiled all proof files and both positive/negative executable replay fixtures:

- Arbitrary-length transitive delegation cannot expand action, security-label or payload-byte authority.
- The executable `CheckedReplay` is *sound and complete* for its sequential unit-debit transition semantics.
- The executable **finite-dimensional weighted** `CheckWeighted` is *sound and complete* for the independent `WeightedRuns` specification, with arbitrary action costs and independent budget/grant/delegation resource dimensions.
- Exact per-resource conservation holds across any finite accepted trace; therefore no admitted trace overspends its initial resources.
- Sticky ancestor revocation blocks descendant admissions in the specified model.
- In the abstract external-witness model, a changed database fingerprint cannot authorize execution against the independently pinned witness; a crash between DB commit and witness publish fails closed.
- `TraceExample.lean` verifies both admitted and refused weighted traces, while `SyntheticGatewayReceipt.lean` checks an accounting projection generated by a separate synthetic Writ/11 gateway instance. The latter's source log is **not independently authenticated by the Lean kernel**.

The proof model assumes sequential atomic debits, trustworthy actor binding, exact identifiers and externally protected witness equality. It does not prove that Python/SQLite follows these assumptions.

It can export operator-only Lean accounting receipts using `writ-host certificate CONFIG`. In its local execution environment, 271 tests and the separate-process rollback demonstration pass. **That full Python runtime is not included in this PR.** Its host-local witness is effective only if the actor able to roll back SQLite cannot also modify or roll back the witness.

## Explicit open proof obligations

1. Formal refinement from the actual compiler, actor authentication, signatures, SQLite transactions, and replay logic into the abstract Lean transition system.
2. Trusted witness root that is independently durable, monotonic, and inaccessible to SQLite rollback attackers.
3. Verified signing/intake/provenance semantics and complete mediation of real agent tools.
4. Novelty and comparative evaluation against existing capability-security systems, third-party review, and deployment threat modelling.

A kernel-checked accounting theorem does not establish truth of scientific premises, physical-world safety, verified declassification, or general AI alignment. No production PCS modules or existing CI definitions are changed, no merges or deployments are authorized.

## Writ/12: verified explicit-provenance and dual-axis release reasoning

Writ/12 adds a new finite `CheckFlow` checker over explicit data-derivation edges in
`formal/Provenance.lean`. Lean proves equivalence to the independent
declarative edge condition, arbitrary-length non-laundering of sensitivity, and
no lower-classification release of a descendant of a higher-classification
ancestor **without declassification**.

`formal/DualBoundary.lean` composes this with `Writ9.SafeChain`, so that
a successful action at a delegated scope implies **both** authorization within
its ancestral scope and source-label compatibility at the sink, *assuming*
the independent monitors receive complete, correct inputs.

A synthetic Writ/12 gateway produces
`formal/GeneratedProvenanceReceipt.lean` (four resources, one multi-parent
derived value), which the exact-head proof workflow checks together with
a positive and deliberately invalid classification fixture.

The complete Writ/12 Python runtime is **not included in this PR**. It adds
`audit provenance;` in both policy syntaxes and independently reconstructs
host-pinned root bytes, deterministic concatenation recipes, exact derived
bytes, joined labels, and transitive lineage before admission and dispatch.
It passed 297 unit tests, 1,500 deterministic graph cases, and separate
process-integrated witness/provenance demonstrations when packaged locally.

**Limitations:** No formal refinement from the Python runtime to these Lean
theorems, no guarantee of complete dataflow observation or arbitrary-model
noninterference, no security against rewriting both trusted pins and values,
and no claim that delegated actions are harmless or scientifically true.

## Writ/13 — Source-anchored proof-carrying execution receipts

The companion **Writ/13 alpha**, delivered separately from this research PR, now offers `witness provenance;`: a domain-separated external witness commitment to the full explicit host source manifest (root byte digests, classifications, ordered derivation inputs, separator digests, and transitive lineage). This closes a concrete prior threat-model gap:

1. Under a root-file substitution attacker who cannot rewrite the external witness, Writ/12 rebuilt new trusted source pins after restart and accepted the replacement as a fresh host intake.
2. Writ/13 refuses that same valid-ledger/changed-root restart, even when the database and its internal signature were not modified.
3. `python scripts/compare_source_restarts_v13.py` in the complete local Writ/13 package reproduces both observations. This protection assumes a witness *outside the attacker's rollback/write domain*.

**Detached receipts:** the operator can independently Ed25519-sign a bounded canonical audit projection. A detached receiver, using a pinned operator public key, checks the signature, policy hash, source-manifest commitment, explicit graph labels, all recorded admissions and ancestor/budget debits, outbox identities, and hash-linked event continuity. The receiver needs neither SQLite nor the gateway's HMAC keys. To prove *freshness* rather than historical authenticity, an independently trusted latest checkpoint must also be supplied. By default, public receipts refuse metadata from internal/secret datasets to avoid revealing low-entropy private values through dictionary attacks on raw SHA digests.

**Lean result:** `formal/AnchoredExecution.lean` is a computable product checker of an *opaque pinned source identity*, explicit-flow label validity, and finite weighted admission traces. Lean 4.28.0 kernel-checks its soundness, completeness, source-bound acceptance, no-overspending, and non-laundering conclusions **relative to their independently stated transition and graph semantics**. `formal/SignedGatewayReceipt.lean` is an actual deterministic source/authority-accounting projection generated after a successful synthetic signed Writ/13 receipt verification. Its accept case and deliberately wrong source-commitment reject case are kernel-checked. The Ed25519 signature and SHA-256 preimage are verified by Python, **not** Lean.

**Independent cross-check:** `source_witness.py` is a separate reference implementation of the explicit root/concat provenance commitment. `test_source_witness.py` checks it against the frozen Writ/13 fixture commitment `35508ee5c4f886ef8a2154b81ce39a5318ec6d0334995d2dc1179087ac78eb8a` and rejects changed roots/recipes, including a hidden source substitution.

The complete Writ/13 runtime (including external Ed25519 receipt verifier) is intentionally **not imported into PCS production**. Neither a kernel-checked projection nor an operator signature establishes honesty of the host, legitimacy of reviewer signers, correctness of arbitrary model behavior, implicit-flow security, real tool effects, or Python-to-Lean refinement. That precise refinement problem and an independent benchmark against Cedar and proof-carrying-agent protocols remain the primary research blockers.

## Writ/14: independently checked effect lifecycles and no automatic ambiguous retry

Writ/13 had a **reproducible semantic defect in its detached receipt receiver**: with the synthetic test operator's legitimate Ed25519 private key, a tester could append a second `delivered` event for the same effect, recompute all hash-link fields and the envelope signature, and have Writ/13 accept the contradictory receipt. This is *not* a cryptographic forgery by an attacker who lacks the signing key. It shows that even authenticated, operator-signed histories need a separate semantic validator. A test operator can also be buggy.

The companion Writ/14 runtime (distributed as a separate alpha release; **not imported into PCS production**) introduces opt-in `audit effects;` (requires `audit authority;` and `witness provenance;`), a new `writ/14` policy identity, and a domain-separated `writ/portable-receipt/2`. The gateway durably records `dispatch_lease` *before* external adapter I/O. Ambiguous adapter exceptions, including an exception after a real effect, and expired worker leases become terminal `uncertain` states; **they are not automatically reexecuted**. The gateway and detached signature verifier independently reconstruct each effect's history and compare it with durable/signed outbox status.

The audited transition rules are:

- `pending --dispatch_lease--> working`
- `working --delivered--> delivered`
- `working --dispatch_uncertain--> uncertain`
- `pending --cancel_effect--> canceled`
- `pending --dispatch_suppressed--> suppressed`
- `pending --dispatch_expired--> expired`

Every terminal state is absorbing. The verifier rejects duplicate delivery, cancellation after delivery, delivery without a lease, and mismatched outbox status, even when an inconsistent test history has been validly re-signed.

**Kernel-checked theorem:** `formal/EffectLifecycle.lean` proves the executable transition function sound and complete against separately stated allowed transitions, finite-trace replay sound and complete, and terminal-state non-reentrance. `formal/TripleSafety.lean` composes that model with the already kernel-checked source-anchored weighted authority and explicit provenance checks, establishing conditional product-checker soundness, completeness and no overspending. `formal/SignedEffectReceipt.lean` was **generated from an actual synthetic Writ/14 signed gateway receipt** after detached verification; Lean checks each resource balance, lease→delivery history, combined acceptance and an impossible post-terminal transition. The [dedicated Writ CI run](https://github.com/Tommaso-R-Marena/proof-carrying-science/actions/runs/42646453323) passed all three source files on Lean 4.28.0.

**Reproducible implementation evidence:** 335/335 full Python tests; 55,987 event sequences (lengths 0–6) matched an independently authored finite-state oracle; clean-installed wheel; separate-process Writ/14 admission→lease→delivery→signed-receipt→Lean-export demonstration; inherited Writ/13 demo still passes. Writ/14's complete runtime and confidential synthetic test signing key were *not* added to the PCS PR.

**Important nonclaims:** Even a formally consistent `delivered` log entry does not prove that the remote tool *physically delivered* anything; that remains a trusted adapter/operator assertion. Writ/14 does **not** establish exactly-once physical effects, trusted operator honesty, complete mediation, remote idempotency, key custody, source provenance authenticity, or Python-to-Lean refinement. Strict effect auditing is opt-in; Writ/13 historical policies retain their weaker receipt semantics unless migrated. The real next milestone is a trusted, independently attested adapter receipt or narrow executable refinement proof.

## Writ/15: independent destination-signed delivery receipts

Writ/14 only checked that a gateway/operator logged a semantically consistent
`delivered` event. **It did not require evidence from the actual destination.**
That was a real assurance gap: a compromised or buggy operator could validly
sign a false delivery history.

**Companion Writ/15 alpha** adds opt-in `audit destination;` (requires
`audit effects;`) and domain-separated `writ/15` policy /
`writ/portable-receipt/3` identities. It provides:

1. A distinct UNIX-domain-socket **receiver process** with its own Ed25519
   signing key, separately from gateway request signing and operator audit
   signing. The receiver verifies the gateway-signed request and its immutable
   policy, source digest, effect ID, route, receiver identity and fresh worker
   lease. No untrusted agent can directly select an arbitrary file path.
2. Receiver-side pinned-directory writes and fsync before signing the
   `durably_written` acknowledgment. The receiver persists a per-effect
   acknowledgment so a different nonce, payload or route cannot later receive
   a conflicting receipt, including after receiver restart.
3. Gateway-side verification of the **independent receiver signature**
   before recording `delivered`. Unacknowledged or ambiguously interrupted
   effects remain `uncertain` without automatic replay.
4. Detached receiver+operator signature verification, with a receiver public
   key **pinned outside** the operator-signed portable receipt. Exact policy,
   source, route, digest, nonce, effect and resource accounting are checked
   before exporting the Lean proof projection.
5. A concrete adversarial regression: a test operator modifies its delivery
   audit log, repairs all hash links, and legitimately re-signs the resulting
   operator receipt. Writ/15 still rejects it because the receiver signature
   does not validate.

### Newly verified Lean source

- `formal/DestinationAttestation.lean`: executable receipt-bound
  state machine, state-safety preservation across arbitrary finite traces,
  no accepted delivered outcome without an exact verified acknowledgment,
  and rejection of bad receiver identity, failed-signature markers and
  post-terminal replay.
- `formal/AttestedProduct.lean`: soundness, completeness and no weighted
  overspending when independent receiver acknowledgment checks compose
  with Writ/13's source-anchored provenance and resource calculus.
- `formal/DestinationReceiptExample.lean`: synthetic operator-and-receiver
  signature verified numeric trace fixture with positive and negative controls.
- `destination_reference.py` and `test_destination_reference.py`: separate
  Python Ed25519 reference checker and adversarial tests, not imported into
  production PCS. The full Writ/15 gateway, receiver service and integration
  harness are in the downloadable companion research source package; this PR
  retains an isolated reviewable verification slice.

The formal source was checked with pinned Lean 4.28.0 in
[Writ proof workflow #58](https://github.com/Tommaso-R-Marena/proof-carrying-science/actions/runs/42655063411).
The comprehensive companion Writ/15 suite passed **343 tests**, the new
separate-process gateway→receiver→detached-verifier demonstration, and
an inherited Writ/14 demonstration. Its public synthetic evidence and
version 15 source archive can be independently inspected.

**Trust assumptions and nonclaims:** The Lean theorem's
`signatureVerified = true` input is supplied by Python's Ed25519 verifier.
The recipient's private key must actually be isolated from gateway/operator
compromise, and the recipient must honestly perform the fsynced operation.
The test uses separate processes and keys but **one test OS user**, so it
does not establish real OS key isolation. A receiver acknowledgment is a
signed statement about a local write, not a mathematical proof of remote
physical delivery or arbitrary real-world safety. No Python/SQLite-to-Lean
refinement theorem, implicit-flow proof, cryptographic theorem, or complete
agent tool mediation is established. Earlier policy versions are unchanged;
production PCS has not been modified, deployed or merged.

## Writ/16: fresh verifier-chosen challenges for receiver-side readback

**Concrete Writ/15 regression, reproduced before editing the receiver:**

1. Present a valid gateway-signed Writ/15 request to a correctly keyed
   receiver. It writes the file and returns an independently signed ACK.
2. Delete `writ-000000000001.bin` but leave
   `writ-000000000001.ack.json` intact.
3. Re-send the exact same authorized request. **Writ/15 returned the same
   signed ACK while the file was missing.**
4. Simply adding a read check to `previous_ack` was insufficient: its old
   broad `except FileNotFoundError` also caught the *missing output file*,
   fell through to the output write path and could silently recreate the
   effect. Writ/16 corrects that exception boundary and refuses the mismatch.

**Companion Writ/16 release (separate from this PR):** Adds opt-in
`audit retention;` (requires Writ/15 destination auditing), a new
`writ/16` policy identity and domain-separated
`writ/portable-receipt/4` signed records. A Writ/16 gateway now requires
receiver-signed **readback evidence after delivery**. For a post-hoc present
state claim, `python -m writlang.portable_receipt verify-live` first verifies
the operator and destination signatures and signed audit/authority history,
then issues a **new verifier-generated random nonce** to the independently
pinned receiver socket. The receiver checks its persisted signed ACK against
its journal, rereads the pinned regular file using no-follow directory-FD
operations, checks byte length/digest, and signs a bounded readback response.

A saved, valid signed ACK is explicitly **not** treated as evidence of current
file presence. A captured older readback response is rejected under a new
nonce. The new receiver signature is bound to that verifier nonce, the exact
saved ACK hash, policy, effect ID, destination route, lease nonce, source
digest and byte count.

**This research PR contains the new Lean model**
`formal/LiveRetention.lean` and the independent adversarial receiver-signature
checker `retention_reference.py` with `test_retention_reference.py`, plus
`formal/LiveRetentionExample.lean`. The Lean model distinguishes the
logical insufficiency of historical signed ACKs from a fresh readback
predicate, states and attempts to prove that a combined checker accepting
source/provenance/weighted authority and fresh readback evidence necessarily
has the exact challenged bytes/receipt/receiver binding, and preserves
the prior no-overspending invariant.

**Implementation results from the independently packaged Writ/16 alpha:**
- 352/352 local Python unit tests PASS (343 inherited plus 9 new);
- new two-process Writ/16 demo PASS, including a fresh verifier challenge,
  deletion and fail-closed refusal with the original ACK still valid, then
  restoration and re-verification;
- existing Writ/15 policy identity remains unchanged; new Writ/16 uses a
  distinct signing domain;
- ordinary historical detached verification is not a current-file claim;
  `verify-live` must reach the independent receiver.

**Formal and threat-model boundaries:** The Lean checker treats
`signatureVerified=true` and `bytesPresent=true` as facts produced by a
trusted receiver/cryptographic-checker boundary. It does *not* prove Python,
Ed25519, OS isolation, receiver honesty, that remote physical delivery happened,
or that bytes will remain present after the challenge. The local same-user
test does not independently isolate receiver keys at the OS level. The
proposal is a narrower **point-in-time readback assurance improvement**, not
a proof of durable storage forever or general AI safety. The proof workflow
must compile the exact Writ/16 Lean file before it can be described as
machine-checked; do not infer that from earlier successful Writ/15 runs.

## Writ/17: write-once effect claims and conservative crash recovery

**New reproducible Writ/16 counterexample:** deliver effect ID 7, delete only
`writ-000000000007.ack.json` while keeping its output, then send a *separately
valid gateway-signed request* for effect ID 7 with a different lease nonce.
The old receiver accepted the equal output bytes and emitted **two distinct,
Ed25519-valid delivery ACKs for the same effect ID**. This is a receiver
journal-loss equivocation attack, **not** a forged receiver signature.

The companion Writ/17 alpha (downloaded separately, not imported into
PCS production) adds `audit linearity;` and a new `writ/17` policy /
`writ/portable-receipt/5` identity. The receiver advertises
`writ/receiver-claim/1` through its signed identity challenge. A strict
gateway refuses any older receiver that cannot prove it implements this
protocol.

The receiver persists a **canonical request-bound first-claim** via fsynced
temporary file, exclusive hardlink and parent-directory fsync **before** the
first output write. A claim binds exact policy, route, payload, effect ID,
lease nonce and receiver identity via the gateway's signed request hash.
Only the first successful reservation may invoke the adapter and publish a
new signed ACK. An existing, identical claim may return an *existing* verified
ACK without reexecuting; if that ACK is missing, the outcome is **uncertain**
and no automatic retry or new ACK is permitted. An output/ACK with no claim
is treated as a possible rollback and refused.

Concrete implementation results from the separately packaged Writ/17:
- **367/367** Python regression tests (352 inherited, 15 new), PASS.
- **597,871** bounded effect-event sequences (length up to 6) evaluated
  against an independent oracle; 39 are valid, zero mismatches or valid
  dual-ACK traces. This is *bounded testing*, not a kernel-checked proof.
- Separate gateway/receiver-process delivery, operator + receiver Ed25519
  receipt verification, live readback, receiver restart and refusal of
  conflicting nonce after deliberate ACK deletion: **PASS**.
- Crash injection before and after the output fsync: the permanent claim
  remains and the receiver refuses automatic reexecution.
- Clean-installed Python wheel and SHA-256 checked source archive.

Reviewable PCS PR code:
- `formal/ReceiverClaim.lean`: candidate non-equivocation proof for
  arbitrary finite transition traces, claim binding preservation, and
  uncertain-state no-retry.
- `receiver_claim_reference_v17.py` and
  `test_receiver_claim_reference_v17.py`: independent, runnable POSIX
  first-claim reference with crash/orphan/concurrent-claim tests.
- `verify_claim_v17.py`: the stand-alone finite oracle.
- Updated dedicated Writ research workflow to run the new Python and Lean
  checks. **Do not report the new Lean candidate as kernel-checked until
  an exact-commit hosted run passes.**

**Limits and prior art:** The durable claim must not itself be rolled back or
silently deleted. A receiver attacker able to remove *claim + output + ACK*
can erase all local evidence, making changed history indistinguishable from
a genuinely new operation absent an **independent non-rollbackable witness**.
The local prototype uses one OS account with separate processes/keys and
does not prove true OS compartmentalization or filesystem hardware behavior.
For general external effects, crash-safe deduplication requires destination
transactionality; Writ/17 deliberately sacrifices availability for safety.
This is closely related to long-established exactly-once and durable RPC
techniques, notably Lee et al.'s RIFL system (SOSP 2015,
https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/papers/rifl.pdf), not a demonstrated
novel consensus or storage algorithm. Neither Writ/17 nor its model proves
Python/SQLite refinement, guaranteed real-world delivery, or general AI
alignment.

## Writ/18: claim-linked readback evidence and a distinct signed protocol

**New confirmed Writ/17 gap.** After a valid first-write reservation, output
delivery and receiver-signed ACK, removing *only*
`writ-<effect-id>.claim.json` left the output and ACK intact. The old Writ/17
receiver's `prove-retention` endpoint verified the output and ACK, then
issued a **fresh receiver-signed retention proof even though the foundational
write-once claim no longer existed**. A valid claim of present bytes was
being mistaken for evidence of an intact linearity reservation.

The companion Writ/18 alpha makes a new opt-in language declaration
`audit claim-readback;` dependent on `audit linearity;`. A distinct
`writ/18` canonical policy and `writ/portable-receipt/6` Ed25519 signing
domain prevent old receipts from satisfying the new semantics. A Writ/18
gateway requires the separately signed receiver identity to advertise both
`writ/receiver-claim/1` and `writ/receiver-claim-readback/1`; it rejects
older receivers. The receiver must authenticate its exact persisted first
claim **at the time of each live readback**, reconstructing the signed
original request from the ACK and the current re-read, digest-checked bytes,
before issuing a v18 `writ/retention-proof/2` under its own signing domain.
The new signed `claim_link` is independently recomputed from the authenticated
ACK's policy, effect, route, source digest, byte length and nonce fields.
The new detached verifier rejects the old v16/17 signed proof format.

This missing-first-claim check is also applied as a **security patch to
existing strict Writ/17 receiver implementations** without changing their
canonical policy hashes. Old policies still receive the old signed proof
format but no longer accept a newly signed readback with a missing claim.

The full research distribution (outside this PR) passes **377/377 tests**
(367 inherited plus 10 new adversarial Writ/18 cases). It includes a passing
separate-process gateway→receiver→dual-signature→fresh-readback
demonstration and an inherited Writ/17 separate-process regression.
The test deliberately deletes and restores the claim under a real receiver
and verifies refusal/recovery, and also covers forged and old-domain
signatures, replayed nonces, symlinked/tampered claims, changed bytes,
older receiver capability mismatch and preserved older policy identities.

**Code in this PCS PR:** `formal/ClaimLinkedReadback.lean`, a *standalone*
candidate Lean model (does not depend on Writ/16's previously unconfirmed Lean
compilation), plus `claim_readback_reference_v18.py` and
`test_claim_readback_reference_v18.py`: an independent Ed25519 verifier
and optional pinned-FD original-claim checker with adversarial tests.
The updated research workflow attempts to kernel-check the exact Lean source.

**Verification status:** The 377 local Python tests and separate-process
demonstrations have passed. Do **not** call the new Writ/18 Lean theorems
kernel checked until an exact-source Lean 4.28 run finishes successfully.
The standalone mathematical checker treats `signatureVerified` and
`claimLinked` as trusted inputs, so even a successful Lean proof would
not establish that Python/SQLite/OS operations refine it.

**Unavoidable limitation:** A receiver adversary that erases every local
claim, output, ACK and rollback witness leaves a state observationally
identical to a never-used receiver. Preventing complete rollback requires a
separately protected non-rollbackable witness; signatures and local files
alone cannot prove history. Writ remains a research prototype rather than
a demonstrated novel consensus protocol or general AI alignment solution.
